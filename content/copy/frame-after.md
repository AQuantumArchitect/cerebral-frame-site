Read it the way the owner would. The **current** tiles are the ones you run the week on. The **stale** tiles each earn one question — who last touched the receiving bench, why the Friday numbers are still built by hand. The **dark** tiles are not failures; they are the map of what nobody is counting, and that is where the next Build goes.

## How the Map runs, day by day

**Days 1–3.** We sit with the operation. The actual inbox, the actual spreadsheet, the person who just knows. We write down what gets asked every week and where the answer actually comes from.

**Days 4–7.** We wire read-only sources — the accounting export, the sheets, the job tickets, email where it has to — into a ledger you own. Nothing new to type into. Nothing writes back.

**Days 8–9.** The first Frame comes up. We walk it with you, tile by tile, and argue about what is really current and what only looks that way.

**Day 10.** The short list: what to build first, what to stop doing, what to write down. One page. Yours.

## Questions owners ask

**What feeds it?** Any system that can export — accounting, point of sale, job tickets, a shared sheet, a mailbox. Nothing new to type into.

**Who owns the data?** You do. The ledger is yours and leaves with you. The model that reads it is ours to build and maintain.

**What happens when a source stops?** That number goes stale, visibly, after a window you set. The Frame never pretends. A stale tile is a question; a dark tile is an admission.

**Is this AI?** The engine underneath is a model we build and maintain, not a chatbot. We use language models as tools around it — to read a messy export, to draft a note — and never as the source of a number.

**What is it not?** A dashboard. A chatbot. An ERP. A replacement for the person who knows the floor.

**Has anyone bought one?** Not yet. The first Frame gets our full attention and a written case study, with the client's permission.
